Table 2 / subjective trends

People are imagining ownership—not admiring mockups.

108 open-field comments
1

Walletability

Flatness, pocket fit, no pocket print, and wallet carry recur without prompting.

“B could fit into my wallet which is huge.”
2

Physical realism

People interrogate capacity, thickness, opening, spilling, resealing, and whether the object could exist.

“feels like something that’s actually real”
3

Category cues

Silhouettes borrow meaning from Zyn, weed gummies, birth control, AirPods, floss, and flash drives.

“The tin version … reminds me of a weed gummy.”
4

Shareability

Mints are social. Count, easy access, and whether another person can grab one matter.

“everyone asks for one”
5

Single-use backlash

Wrappers cue waste, noise, litter, and non-resealable use.

“I don’t want to throw away the wrapper.”

Recommended next move

Stop generating broad concepts. Isolate the winning attributes.

Next-test hypothesis
A

Architecture × visual language

Put Slim File’s black/premium expression onto Wallet Strip Card’s clearer wallet/dispensing proposition.

B

Strip vs. tablet, same container

Hold package constant so votes stop mixing consumable preference with industrial-design preference.

C

Prove mechanism + capacity

Show refill, count, dispensing, resealing, and dimensions so realism is designed—not imagined.

The synthesis hypothesis

03’s product logic + 05’s desirability, without Zyn/weed cues, keychains, or single-use wrappers.
